Friendsgiving 2026 — After Action Review

Setup and logistics · November 21, 2026

What worked

  • Three 8-foot tables provided adequate seating and setup space for 26 guests.

What to improve next time

  • Finalize the guest list before purchasing or renting tables. Confirm the final headcount first, then determine table and chair quantities.
  • Verify chair quantities on delivery. Two chairs were missing; count and inspect all chairs immediately against the order.
  • Sort setup materials before helpers arrive. Put items into separate labeled boxes or piles by purpose (table settings, linens, candles/decorations, serving supplies, etc.) so everyone knows where each item belongs.
  • Avoid staging everything by the door until the day before. Materials were left by the door until the helpers arrived; sort and label them earlier so setup can begin immediately.
  • Buy craft and floral supplies at least one day in advance. Purchase everything needed for delegated crafts, including wax seals and flower arrangements, before helpers arrive. Trader Joe’s had enough flowers on the event day, but relying on same-day availability was risky.
  • Test candle fit and stability before the event. The ordered candles were too small for the hurricanes, tilted and tipped over, and left soot on the glass. Museum glue did not reliably secure them. Next time, measure the hurricane and candle base before ordering; use a correctly sized, stable, noncombustible candle holder or fitted cup rather than adhesive, and test the arrangement ahead of time. Use flameless candles if a secure fit is not possible.

Food quantities, menu & leftovers (26 guests)

Overall: Too much food, especially pie, duplicate mac and cheese, breads and meat. Buy take-home containers ahead of time and make them available before guests leave.

  • Stuffing: Jasmine’s stuffing was the right quantity, with only a little left over. Repeat.
  • Sweet potato casserole: Keep Chef Nam’s version.
  • Mac and cheese: Paul brought one and the hosts made another, resulting in too much. Afghan mac and cheese was also good. Plan a maximum of two mac and cheese dishes, coordinate who brings them, and avoid unplanned duplicates.
  • Casseroles: Two green bean casseroles were too many; keep one green bean casserole and one sweet potato casserole.
  • Creamy corn: Keep the Costco creamy sweet corn.
  • Cranberry sauce: Keep Sophia’s homemade cranberry sauce.
  • Breads: Extra Costco focaccia was unnecessary. Reduce bread; Costco carries Hawaiian rolls, which can be purchased there rather than separately ahead of time.
  • Gravy: Prepare earlier. The Costco two-pack was more than needed; buy less.
  • Vegetables: Ashley’s Brussels sprouts and vegetables were popular; keep. Sophia’s carrots were also a favorite; keep.
  • Mashed potatoes: Keep Jordan’s recipe.
  • Applesauce: Keep the applesauce setup/configuration.
  • Proteins: The honey baked ham was excellent; keep one ham. Two turkeys plus a ham plus Costco turkey legs were excessive. For a similar gathering, consider the Costco turkey-leg pack and one ham instead, but confirm portion sizes and dietary preferences before finalizing.
  • Serving: Assign someone to carve the ham so serving is easier.
  • Salads: Two salads felt slightly excessive, but were acceptable; consider one or two depending on the final menu.
  • Dessert overbuy: Hosts bought two large Costco apple pies and two pumpkin pies; Sarah and Shannon each brought pecan pie, Ashley brought another pie, two lemon pies were purchased, and Kayla brought Oreo cheesecake. Far too much dessert for 26 guests.
  • Next dessert menu: Keep Kayla’s Oreo cheesecake, one apple pie, one pumpkin pie, one pecan pie, and two ice creams. Coordinate contributions so no extra pies arrive. Have ice cream scoopers ready.
  • Serving utensils: The utensils purchased from Costco and Amazon combined were not enough for all sides. Count every dish and provide a dedicated serving spoon, ladle, tongs or knife as appropriate, with spares.
  • Pre-label serving setup: Before guests/helpers arrive, assign and label each serving dish, platter, utensil and its table position by food item. Lay everything out in advance to avoid the day-of scramble over which dish to use and whether enough equipment is available.
  • Leftovers: Purchase plenty of disposable take-home containers in advance and set up a clearly marked leftover packing station.
